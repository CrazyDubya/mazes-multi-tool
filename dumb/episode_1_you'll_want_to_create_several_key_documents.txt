To support the story in Episode 1, you'll want to create several key documents. Here are some essential documents that will help you plan a compelling and engaging Episode 1:

### 1. **Episode Outline**

A detailed outline of the episode's structure, including:

* Act 1 setup
	+ Introduce main character and setting
	+ Establish tone and mood
* Inciting Incident
	+ Event that sets the story in motion
	+ Raises stakes or creates conflict
* Rising Action
	+ Series of events that build tension and create obstacles for the protagonist
	+ Develops characters and plot
* Climax
	+ Most intense or critical moment in the episode
	+ Resolves the main conflict or question
* Falling Action
	+ Consequences of the climax
	+ Sets up the resolution
* Resolution
	+ Ties up loose ends
	+ Provides a sense of closure

### 2. **Character Arcs**

Updates on your main characters' development and growth, including:

* Current state
	+ Physical appearance, tone, and attitude
	+ Skills and abilities
	+ Relationships with other characters
* Goals and motivations
	+ What the character wants to achieve
	+ Why they want it
	+ Obstacles or challenges that stand in their way
* Conflict or challenges
	+ Internal struggles (e.g., fears, doubts)
	+ External obstacles (e.g., enemies, environment)
	+ Potential outcomes or resolutions

### 3. **Scene List**

A list of key scenes that will take place in Episode 1, including:

* Dialogue-driven scenes:
	+ Key conversations and interactions
	+ Reveals character traits or backstory
	+ Advances the plot
* Action sequences:
	+ Physical confrontations or chases
	+ Environmental hazards or obstacles
	+ High-stakes moments
* Emotional moments:
	+ Character reveals or insights
	+ Intimate relationships or bonding
	+ Heartfelt moments of connection

### 4. **Thematic Guide**

An outline of the episode's themes and ideas, including:

* Main theme(s):
	+ Central idea or message
	+ Explored through character development, plot, and dialogue
* Supporting themes or subplots:
	+ Secondary ideas that enhance the main theme
	+ Character-driven stories within the larger narrative
* Tone and mood:
	+ Atmosphere and feeling of each scene
	+ Shifts in tone throughout the episode

### 5. **Research Notes**

Any research or background information that will be needed to inform the story, such as:

* World-building details:
	+ Establishing the setting and environment
	+ Defining rules or laws of the world
* Historical or cultural context:
	+ Influences on characters' backgrounds or motivations
	+ Relevant events or era
* Character-specific traits or abilities:
	+ Unique skills or talents
	+ Quirks, habits, or mannerisms

**Supporting Documents**

Additionally, you may also want to create:

1. **Character Profiles**: Detailed profiles of each main character, including physical appearance, background and history, skills and abilities, and relationships with other characters.
2. **Episode Notes**: A list of key events, dialogue, or plot points that will take place in Episode 1.
3. **Storyboard**: A visual representation of the episode's scenes, including character poses and expressions, setting and environment, and action sequences.

**Tips for Creating Documents**

1. **Keep it Concise**: Focus on creating clear, concise documents that are easy to read and understand.
2. **Use Visual Aids**: Incorporate visual aids like diagrams, flowcharts, or mind maps to help illustrate complex concepts or plot points.
3. **Collaborate with Team Members**: Share your documents with team members and ask for feedback to ensure everyone is on the same page.

By creating these supporting documents, you'll be able to plan a compelling and engaging Episode 1 that sets the stage for the rest of the series.
